STEP 1 — COMPLETE UNIT 4A MAP

1.1 Unit Identification

Course: American English File 4, 3rd Edition

Level: Level 4 / Upper-intermediate

Unit: Unit 4

Lesson: 4A

Lesson title: Stormy Weather

1.2 Central Theme

The lesson develops a connected thematic field around:

  • weather;
  • environmental behavior;
  • the future;
  • predictions;
  • climate change;
  • extreme weather;
  • people's relationship with weather;
  • future environmental developments.

The central classroom idea is:

How might weather, the environment, and people's lives change in the future?

1.3 Main Language Areas

Grammar

  • Future Perfect
  • Future Continuous

Vocabulary

  • weather-related vocabulary;
  • environmental vocabulary;
  • descriptive language for weather;
  • modifiers with strong adjectives.

Pronunciation

  • target vowel sounds in the relevant vocabulary.

Prediction Language

  • definitely;
  • probably;
  • likely;
  • unlikely.

1.6 Core Grammar Contrast

The central grammar contrast is:

Future Perfect

will have + past participle

Meaning:

an action will be completed before a future reference point.

Example:

By 2045, many countries will have changed the way they produce energy.

Future Continuous

will be + verb-ing

Meaning:

an activity will be in progress at a particular future time.

Example:

In 2045, people will be using more renewable energy.

1.7 Core Communicative Outcome

Students should ultimately be able to talk about future developments using the target language naturally.

A successful final performance may sound like:

By 2045, many cities will have changed the way they use energy.

In 2045, people will be using more renewable energy.

I think this will probably make cities cleaner, although some areas may still have extremely hot summers.

The important outcome is:

language used for communication rather than grammar used only for completion of exercises.

STEP 2 — LEARNING OBJECTIVES

2.1 Main Aim

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to discuss future developments related to weather, the environment, and climate change using:

  • Future Perfect;
  • Future Continuous;
  • prediction language;
  • relevant weather and environmental vocabulary;
  • natural descriptive language.

2.2 Grammar Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. recognize Future Perfect;
  2. recognize Future Continuous;
  3. understand the difference in meaning;
  4. form both structures accurately;
  5. use Future Perfect for completed actions before a future point;
  6. use Future Continuous for actions in progress at a future point;
  7. choose the appropriate form based on meaning rather than simply on a future time expression;
  8. produce original examples.

2.3 Vocabulary Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. recognize weather-related vocabulary;
  2. understand weather vocabulary in context;
  3. pronounce key items;
  4. retrieve vocabulary without the book;
  5. use weather vocabulary in sentences;
  6. use weather vocabulary in personal communication;
  7. use stronger descriptive language where appropriate.

2.4 Pronunciation Objectives

Students will:

  1. listen for the target vowel sounds;
  2. notice the relevant sound distinctions;
  3. discriminate between sounds;
  4. produce the target sounds more accurately;
  5. use the target words in meaningful speech.

2.5 Reading Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. predict content from the topic;
  2. read for gist;
  3. identify specific information;
  4. locate evidence in the text;
  5. connect the reading to their own experience.

2.6 Listening Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. predict content before listening;
  2. listen for gist;
  3. identify key information;
  4. listen for specific details;
  5. discuss information from the recording.

2.7 Speaking Objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. discuss their experience of weather;
  2. describe preferred and disliked weather;
  3. discuss extreme weather;
  4. discuss climate change;
  5. make predictions;
  6. express different degrees of certainty;
  7. use Future Perfect and Future Continuous in communication;
  8. respond to a partner and maintain interaction.

2.8 Higher-Level Learning Objective

The deeper objective is not:

"Can the student define Future Perfect?"

It is:

Can the student use the language to communicate a meaningful idea about the future?

2.9 Student-Friendly Can-Do Statements

By the end of the lesson:

I can...

☐ talk about future predictions.

☐ use Future Perfect.

☐ use Future Continuous.

☐ explain the difference between the two forms.

☐ describe weather.

☐ talk about climate change.

☐ understand information about climate change.

☐ understand information about a meteorologist's work.

☐ express how certain I am about a prediction.

☐ talk about my own experience with weather.

STAGE 1 — LEAD-IN & ENGAGEMENT

Time

8 minutes

Objective

Activate students' existing knowledge about:

  • weather;
  • environment;
  • environmental behavior.

Create immediate personal engagement.

Interaction

T → Ss → Pairs

Procedure

Step 1 — Visual / verbal trigger

Write:

WEATHER

Ask:

What kind of weather do you like?

What kind of weather do you hate?

Students think individually for a few seconds.

Then compare answers with a partner.

Step 2 — Expand the topic

Write:

How does weather affect your life?

Elicit ideas such as:

  • mood;
  • travel;
  • work;
  • exercise;
  • transportation;
  • clothes;
  • sleep;
  • outdoor activities.

Do not teach vocabulary formally yet.

Step 3 — Connect to environment

Ask:

Are you environmentally friendly?

Students discuss briefly.

Then:

What do environmentally friendly people do?

Possible ideas:

  • recycle;
  • use public transportation;
  • save water;
  • save energy;
  • avoid unnecessary plastic;
  • reuse things.

ICQs

Are you writing a paragraph?

No.

Are you talking to your partner?

Yes.

Feedback

Take only a few answers.

Do not correct every grammatical error.

Transition

Say:

Now let's think about how our lives — and our environment — might be different in the future.

STAGE 2 — ENVIRONMENTAL CONTEXT

Time

10 minutes

Objective

Introduce the environmental context and prepare students for future predictions.

Procedure

Use the environmental-friendliness questionnaire from the textbook.

Students:

  1. consider their environmental values;
  2. consider their environmental actions;
  3. compare their results;
  4. discuss examples with a partner.

Teacher Role

Monitor rather than interrupt.

Listen for:

  • useful vocabulary;
  • communication problems;
  • interesting opinions;
  • examples that can later be reused.

Pair Discussion

Do you think you are environmentally friendly?

Which environmental actions are easy for you?

Which ones are difficult?

Why?

Feedback

Ask a few students to report something interesting about their partner.

Useful reporting language:

My partner thinks...

He/She usually...

Transition

Write:

20 YEARS FROM NOW

Ask:

What will be different?

STAGE 3 — FUTURE PREDICTIONS

Time

8 minutes

Objective

Create a genuine communicative need for future forms before formal grammar analysis.

Procedure

Put students in pairs.

Give them these areas:

ENERGY
TRANSPORTATION
FOOD & WATER
WASTE
WEATHER

Ask them to make predictions.

Possible examples:

People will use more renewable energy.

Cars will become more efficient.

The weather will become more extreme.

Do not formally teach Future Perfect / Future Continuous yet.

Challenge Questions

Write:

What will have changed by 2045?

Then:

What will people be doing in 2045?

Ask students to compare the two questions.

STAGE 4 — GRAMMAR DISCOVERY

Time

15 minutes

Objective

Students discover the meaning and form of Future Perfect and Future Continuous from context.

PART A — FUTURE PERFECT

Write:

By 2045, many countries will have changed the way they produce energy.

Ask:

CCQ 1

Is the change happening now?

No.

CCQ 2

Is the change completed before 2045?

Yes.

CCQ 3

Are we looking back from a future point?

Yes.

Highlight:

will have + past participle

Concept

NOW -------------------- 2045
             ↑
       completed action

Core Meaning

An action will be completed before a specific point in the future.

PART B — FUTURE CONTINUOUS

Write:

In 20 years, people will be using more renewable energy.

Ask:

CCQ 1

Is the activity happening now?

No.

CCQ 2

Will the activity be in progress at that future time?

Yes.

Highlight:

will be + verb-ing

Concept

NOW -------------------- 2045
                         ↑
                   action in progress

Core Meaning

An action will be in progress at a particular time in the future.

PART C — CONTRAST

Board:

FUTURE PERFECT

will have + past participle
        ↓
completed by a future point


FUTURE CONTINUOUS

will be + -ing
        ↓
in progress at a future point

Ask students to explain the difference in their own words.

STAGE 5 — CONTROLLED → GUIDED → MEANINGFUL PRACTICE

Time

15 minutes

Objective

Move students from grammatical accuracy toward meaningful use.

Part A — Controlled Practice

Students complete the textbook grammar activity.

Focus on:

  • correct auxiliary;
  • correct verb form;
  • correct tense choice.

Teacher monitors.

Part B — Pair Check

Students compare answers.

Instruction:

Don't just compare the words. Explain why you chose the tense.

Part C — Teacher Feedback

Check answers with the class.

Ask:

Why Future Perfect?

or:

Why Future Continuous?

rather than only:

Correct.

STAGE 5B — GUIDED PRODUCTION

Time

7 minutes

Give students:

By 2045, people will have...

By 2045, governments will have...

In 20 years, people will be...

In 20 years, cities will be...

Students complete the sentences.

Peer Check

Partner asks:

Why did you use Future Perfect / Future Continuous?

STAGE 6 — WEATHER VOCABULARY

Time

10 minutes

Objective

Build the lexical resources students need to describe weather naturally.

Procedure

Use the weather vocabulary from the textbook.

Step 1 — Identify

Students identify the weather conditions.

Step 2 — Meaning

Students explain or match meanings.

Step 3 — Categorize

Where useful, organize vocabulary into:

PRECIPITATION
rain
snow
etc.

TEMPERATURE
hot
freezing
etc.

WIND / STORMS
storm
thunder
etc.

GENERAL CONDITIONS
humid
clear
cloudy
etc.

Use categories only where appropriate to the actual vocabulary.

Step 4 — Personalization

Ask:

Which of these weather conditions do you experience most often?

Which do you dislike most?

Which have you experienced recently?

STAGE 7 — PRONUNCIATION

Time

8 minutes

Objective

Help students notice and produce the target vowel sounds.

Procedure

Step 1 — Listen

Students listen to the target words.

Step 2 — Notice

Ask:

Which vowel sound do you hear?

Step 3 — Classify

Students put words into the appropriate sound groups.

Step 4 — Repeat

Sequence:

whole class
   ↓
individual students
   ↓
pairs

Teaching Principle

Do not make pronunciation a long isolated repetition drill.

Use:

HEAR
 ↓
NOTICE
 ↓
DISTINGUISH
 ↓
PRODUCE
 ↓
USE

STAGE 8 — READING

Climate Stories Project

Time

15 minutes

Objective

Students understand specific information about how climate change affects people's lives in different places.

PRE-READING

Step 1 — Prediction

Use the images and title.

Ask:

What do you think these stories are about?

How can climate change affect people's daily lives?

Students predict in pairs.

WHILE READING — FIRST READING

Students read quickly for the general idea.

Ask:

What is the main purpose of these stories?

Do not allow students to translate every sentence.

SECOND READING

Students read again for specific information.

They identify:

  • who the person is;
  • where they live;
  • what has changed;
  • how the change affects their life.

PAIR CHECK

Students compare answers.

Teacher monitors.

FEEDBACK

Ask:

Where did you find the answer?

This encourages evidence-based reading.

PERSONALIZATION

Ask:

Have you noticed any similar changes in your own area?

Students discuss with a partner.

STAGE 9 — LISTENING

A Meteorologist's Job

Time

12 minutes

Objective

Students identify key information and specific details in a discussion about meteorology, weather forecasting, and climate change.

PRE-LISTENING

Ask:

What does a meteorologist do?

Is a meteorologist the same as a TV weather reporter?

How accurate are weather forecasts?

Can we predict the weather several months from now?

Students discuss briefly.

FIRST LISTENING — GIST

Instruction:

Listen for the general idea. Don't try to write everything.

Question:

What is the main topic?

SECOND LISTENING — SPECIFIC INFORMATION

Students listen again and identify the examples / information required by the textbook task.

PAIR CHECK

Students compare answers.

WHOLE-CLASS FEEDBACK

Check answers.

Replay short sections when useful rather than automatically replaying the entire recording.

POST-LISTENING

Ask:

Did anything the meteorologist said surprise you?

Has your opinion about weather forecasting changed?

STAGE 10 — LANGUAGE-TO-COMMUNICATION TRANSFER

Time

7 minutes

Objective

Integrate the language and information from the lesson.

Board

WEATHER
CLIMATE CHANGE
THE FUTURE

Students produce at least one statement about each.

Required Language

Students should use:

One Future Perfect sentence

By 2045, ...

One Future Continuous sentence

In 20 years, ...

One prediction marker

probably / definitely / likely / unlikely

One weather expression

From the vocabulary studied in the lesson.

STAGE 11 — FINAL SPEAKING

Time

12 minutes

Objective

Provide the final communicative outcome.

ROUND 1 — PERSONAL WEATHER

Students discuss:

What's your favorite kind of weather?

What's your least favorite?

How does weather affect your mood?

ROUND 2 — EXTREME WEATHER

Ask:

Have you ever experienced extreme weather?

Students describe the experience.

Possible areas:

  • heavy rain;
  • flood;
  • hurricane;
  • freezing weather;
  • extreme heat;
  • thunderstorm.

ROUND 3 — CLIMATE CHANGE

Ask:

What do you think the weather will be like in 20 years?

Students must use future language.

ROUND 4 — STRONG ADJECTIVES

Review modifiers from the lesson:

really

extremely

incredibly

unbelievably

and:

absolutely + strong adjective

Example:

absolutely freezing

Students improve simple descriptions.

FINAL COMMUNICATIVE TASK

Weather Forecast from the Future

Students work in pairs.

Student A is a future weather/environment reporter.

Student B is an interviewer.

They imagine they are reporting from the year 2045.

They must discuss:

  1. weather;
  2. transportation;
  3. energy;
  4. environmental conditions;
  5. climate change;
  6. people's daily lives.

They must use:

  • Future Perfect;
  • Future Continuous;
  • prediction language;
  • weather vocabulary;
  • at least one strong adjective + modifier.

Example

By 2045, many cities will have changed the way they use energy.

People will be using more renewable sources.

Summers will probably be much hotter.

Some areas will have experienced more extreme weather.

The weather will be incredibly unpredictable.

TEACHER MONITORING

Do not interrupt every error.

Record examples under:

GOOD LANGUAGE

ERRORS TO REVIEW

INTERESTING IDEAS

DELAYED ERROR CORRECTION

Example:

By 2045, people will have using more solar energy.

Ask:

Is this correct?

Students correct:

By 2045, people will have used more solar energy.

Another:

In 2045, people will have using electric cars.

Students identify:

will be using

STAGE 12 — CONSOLIDATION

Time

5 minutes

Ask students individually to complete:

1

By 2045, I think people will have...

2

In 20 years, people will be...

3

The weather in my area will probably...

4

One environmental change I expect is...

Students share one answer with a partner.

EXIT CHECK

Question 1

What's the main difference between:

will have done

and:

will be doing?

Expected:

completed before a future point vs. in progress at a future point.

Question 2

Give one weather word you learned / reviewed.

Question 3

Make one prediction about the future.

HOMEWORK

Option A — Personal Prediction

Write 8–10 sentences about life in 2045.

Requirements:

  • at least 3 Future Perfect sentences;
  • at least 3 Future Continuous sentences;
  • at least 2 prediction expressions;
  • at least 4 weather/environment words.

Option B — A Weather Report from 2045

Write a short future weather/environment report as if you were a reporter in 2045.

Include:

  • weather;
  • climate;
  • transportation;
  • energy;
  • people's lives.

ANTICIPATED PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS

Problem 1 — Students confuse the two future forms

Solution

Return to the timeline:

Future Perfect
→ completed by a future point

Future Continuous
→ happening around / at a future point

Use CCQs rather than another long explanation.

Problem 2 — Students use "will" for everything

Ask:

Is the action completed by that future time?

or:

Is the action in progress at that future time?

Problem 3 — Students translate every word in reading

Set a clear reading purpose:

Read for the general idea.

Then:

Read again for specific information.

Problem 4 — Listening becomes a memory test

Give a clear purpose before each listening:

First listening: general idea.

Second listening: specific information.

Replay short sections where useful.

Problem 5 — Speaking becomes a grammar exercise

During final speaking:

  1. prioritize fluency;
  2. prioritize meaning;
  3. prioritize communication.

Record errors and use delayed correction.

Problem 6 — Strong adjectives become a memorization exercise

Use them inside meaningful weather descriptions.

Instead of:

It was very cold.

encourage:

It was absolutely freezing.

5.1 GRAMMAR TEACHING SUPPORT

A. Future Perfect

Form

Subject
+

+

Example

Negative

Subject
+

Question

+
subject
+

+
past participle?

Example:

Core Meaning

Future Perfect expresses an action or state viewed as completed before a particular future reference point.

NOW ---------------------- FUTURE POINT
                              2045
                         ↑
                    action completed

Key Time Expressions

  • by 2045;
  • by then;
  • by that time;
  • by the end of the century.

The important semantic relationship is:

completion before a future point

5.2 FUTURE PERFECT — CCQs

Target:

By 2045, people will have changed the way they produce energy.

Ask:

Is the change happening now?

No.

Is 2045 in the future?

Yes.

Will the change be complete by 2045?

Yes.

Are we looking at the action from a future point?

Yes.

5.3 FUTURE PERFECT — COMMON ERRORS

Error 1

By 2045, people will have change...

Correction:

will have changed

Error 2

By 2045, people will have changing...

Correction:

will have changed

Error 3

By 2045, people will changed...

Correction:

will have changed

Error 4

Students use Future Perfect whenever they see a future date.

Solution

Ask:

Is the action completed by that time?

If not, Future Perfect may not be appropriate.

5.4 FUTURE CONTINUOUS

Form

Subject
+
will be
+
verb-ing

Example:

Negative

Subject
+
won't be
+
verb-ing

Example:

Question

Will
+
subject
+
be
+
verb-ing?

Example:

Will people be using more electric vehicles?

5.5 FUTURE CONTINUOUS — CORE MEANING

The Future Continuous presents an activity as in progress at a particular future time.

NOW ---------------------- FUTURE POINT
                              2045
                         ↑
                    action in progress

The focus is not on completion.

The focus is:

What will be happening at that time?

5.6 FUTURE CONTINUOUS — CCQs

Target:

In 2045, people will be using more renewable energy.

Ask:

Is the activity happening now?

No.

Is it related to a future time?

Yes.

Will the activity be in progress at that time?

Yes.

Does the sentence say the activity will be finished in 2045?

No.

5.7 FUTURE PERFECT vs FUTURE CONTINUOUS

Future Perfect Future Continuous
will have + past participle will be + -ing
completed before a future point in progress at a future point
focus = completion/result focus = activity/process
By 2045... In 2045...

Concept Check

Question A

By 2045, what will people have changed?

Focus:

completed changes

Question B

In 2045, what will people be doing?

Focus:

activities in progress

This contrast is more useful than asking students to memorize two formulas.

5.9 PREDICTION LANGUAGE — TEACHING POINT

Do not turn this into a vocabulary memorization activity.

The communicative purpose is:

How certain am I about my prediction?

Ask:

Are you 100% sure?

Are you fairly sure?

Are you only guessing?

5.18 ERROR CORRECTION SYSTEM

Category A — Immediate correction

Correct immediately when:

  • the error blocks communication;
  • the target language is being explicitly practiced;
  • the error is repeated by many students.

Category B — Delayed correction

Record and correct later when:

  • students are doing fluency work;
  • communication is successful;
  • interruption would damage fluency.

Category C — Ignore for now

If the error:

  • is minor;
  • does not affect meaning;
  • is outside today's main objectives;

do not necessarily stop the student.

5.19 STRONG ADJECTIVES + MODIFIERS

The final speaking section can reinforce natural description.

Examples include:

freezing

rather than simply:

very cold

Useful modifiers include:

really

extremely

incredibly

unbelievably

and:

absolutely + strong adjective

Example:

absolutely freezing

5.20 TEACHING PRINCIPLE

Do not teach:

"strong adjectives = stronger vocabulary"

only as a rule.

Instead:

It was very cold.

↓

It was absolutely freezing.

Ask:

Which sounds stronger?

Which sounds more expressive?

7.3 MAIN AIM

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to discuss future developments related to weather, the environment, and climate change using Future Perfect, Future Continuous, prediction language, and relevant vocabulary.

7.4 SUB-AIMS

Students will also:

  1. develop weather vocabulary;
  2. improve recognition and production of target vowel sounds;
  3. develop reading for gist and specific information;
  4. develop listening for gist and specific information;
  5. use modifiers with strong adjectives;
  6. improve fluency and interaction in discussion.

7.5 ASSUMPTIONS

Students are assumed to:

  • know the basic future form with will;
  • have some experience discussing weather;
  • understand basic environmental vocabulary;
  • have enough language to participate in pair discussion.

7.6 POTENTIAL PROBLEMS

Grammar

Students may confuse:

will have done

with:

will be doing.

Vocabulary

Some weather terms may be unfamiliar.

Pronunciation

Students may not clearly distinguish the target vowel sounds.

Reading

Students may try to translate everything.

Listening

Students may attempt to understand every word.

Speaking

Students may focus too heavily on grammatical accuracy.

7.7 SOLUTIONS

Grammar
→ timelines + CCQs

Vocabulary
→ context + examples + personalization

Pronunciation
→ listen → notice → discriminate → produce

Reading
→ gist → details

Listening
→ gist → specific information

Speaking
→ fluency first + delayed correction

Can the student use the language to communicate an idea about the future?

By 2045, many cities will have changed the way they use energy.

In 2045, people will be using more renewable energy.

I think this will probably make cities cleaner, although some areas may still have extremely hot summers.
